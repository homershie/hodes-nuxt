  <p><i>«Drink wine and sing—life is short as morning dew. The days gone by are many.»</i></p>
  <p>
    Some experts call our generation—those in their twenties—Gen Z, the post-90s generation, or digital natives. Some feel lost about the future, unable to find purpose, unmotivated by anything; others are discouraged by the reality of housing and car prices. So we focus on enjoying the present: splurging on an NT$1,000 meal as soon as we get paid; buying expensive clothes to flaunt our taste; snapping Instagram-worthy shots at popular spots to show family and friends we're doing fine. This sense of emptiness is the very ground from which Vaporwave—both as music and visual style—grows. Life's short—why so serious?
  </p>
  <figure class="image">
    <img src="https://r2bucket.homershie.com/assets/imgs/blog/Vapowave/02.jpg" alt="Way out" loading="lazy" />

  <h2>What is Vaporwave?</h2>
  <p>
    Vaporwave emerged online, first gaining traction on Tumblr, drawing heavily on popular culture from the 70s through the 90s. As a music genre, like EDM and Trap, it offers a relaxed, immersive chill—aligned with the post-90s ethos of living for the moment and the escapist feel of being lost in the now. Here we won't dwell on the music; we'll focus on the visual side.
  </p>
  <p>
    Visually, Vaporwave creates a dreamy, bubble-like sense of disillusionment—its tipsy style quickly caught on among young audiences. It feels retro-futuristic, laid-back, jazzy, lo-fi, avant-garde, psychedelic, relaxing, humanistic, sentimental, romantic, nihilistic, dystopian, and uncanny. It blends elements of Glitch Art, Pixel Art, Pop Art, Cyberpunk, and Surrealism. Visually it's a strong mix of old and new, full of tension and contrast. Common Vaporwave motifs include:
  </p>

  <h3>1. Heavy use of magenta and purple, or pastel palettes</h3>
  <p>
    Magenta and purples evoke a dreamlike, hyperreal virtuality; high-brightness, low-saturation pinks add a relaxing, hypnotic effect. This can be mistaken for Cyberpunk, but the lineage is different.
  </p>

  <p>

  <h2>Life is short—seize the day</h2>
  <p>
    Some say Vaporwave is nostalgia for capitalism’s golden age; others see it as satire of a high-speed society that has lost touch with what’s real and simple. I tend to see it as an extension of postmodern nihilism.
  </p>
  <p>
    Vaporwave offers a metaphysical immersion, a psychedelic escape from reality. Its strong visual language quickly gained popularity in subculture, and even the capitalist world it often satirizes has absorbed the style for marketing—which brings to mind a scene from <i>Joker</i>: the elite, embodiment of capitalism, laughing and clapping in a cinema at <i>Modern Times</i>, a film that mocks that very class.
  </p>
  <p>
    Setting aside its deeper meaning, in an era when geometric elements feel overused, Vaporwave suggests another creative angle through a more complex, organic lens—or another way of valuing life. I hope this tipsy style gains more acceptance; I once had a proposal turned down for using it 😬
  </p>
