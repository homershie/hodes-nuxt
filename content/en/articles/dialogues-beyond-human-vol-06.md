From age 23 to 30, I always thought of myself as an explorer wandering across a wasteland. I called myself an "active nihilist," sneered at money, remained non-committal about the future, and drifted aimlessly under the guise of "freelancing." Until reality's hammer fell — I discovered that the so-called freedom was, in essence, an escape from the responsibility of existence.

## I. The Misread Exploration: The "Pseudo-INTP" Under Nihilism

During the long vacation of my twenties, I had fallen into a deep nihilism. I couldn't see the meaning of making money, couldn't afford a home, so I chose the path of least resistance: as long as I didn't affect others, I'd play in my own little world. Back then I always thought I was an INTP, believing the only goal in life was "exploration" and "understanding."

But this kind of exploration often came with a cost. Because I lacked stable financial defenses, my life was like a castle built on sand. Gemini precisely identified my blind spot in our conversation: the "aimless drifting" of that period was actually a long, painful tug-of-war between my INTJ instincts and the nihilism of reality. I thought I was playing, but deep inside I was actually generating anxiety projections from a lack of "output" and "construction."

## II. The Collision of Values: When "Inner Abundance" Meets "Survival Anxiety"

My awakening was not sudden — it came through two devastating moments of clarity.

The first turning point happened in the previous relationship. I suddenly realized that my financial situation couldn't handle the expenses of two people at once. That sense of defeat — "wanting to take care of someone but being powerless to do so" — became the starting point for my decision to set aside design and transition into frontend development. Freedom of the soul, without the armor of material security, is extremely fragile in the face of reality.

The second turning point happened during the collision of this most recent relationship. This was more like a "second lashing" for me — the classic "self-actualization first" vs. "security first" values war.

For her, who came from a traditional middle-class family and pursued stable and conventional paths, my kind of "unconventional" exploration was not courage — it was an irresponsible "playing with fire." When she accused me of "financial disarray" and "no sense of life crisis," although I felt wronged, that destructive clarity struck again. No matter how hard I worked to optimize myself, refine my interests, and discipline myself through exercise, as long as I lacked that "physical armor" — a stable economic baseline and social coordinates — I would become the source of the other person's anxiety.

I also began to admit that a large part of my love of novelty came from skill anxiety accumulated through years of freelancing — fear of being eliminated. But I shouldn't use that anxiety to demand that my partner be the same as me. In adult intimate relationships, financial capability is not just a number; it is a shield that protects the other person from the storms of life. When I was fixated on my own "positioning" and exploration, my instability was slowly, bit by bit, stripping away the other person's sense of security.

## III. The Existential Turn: Evolving from "Exploration" to "Creation"

Through these episodes of acute pain and my desperate self-exploration, I reconfirmed my true nature — I am not actually an explorer (INTP), but a builder (INTJ).

My obsession with side projects like AniDaze and MemeDam is not mere interest — it is my way of fighting nihilism: defining my value through "creation." Combining frontend technology, design thinking, and AI tools, if the world has no preset script, then I will create proof of my existence through "products that solve problems."

I began to understand that **a stable income is not a compromise with reality — it is a necessary foundation for sustaining the desire to create.** The freedom that comes with financial means is true freedom. This is consistent with the thread from vol.01's discussion of "the power to choose your constraints" — accepting the rules of the game is not surrender; it's deciding how to play after seeing clearly.

## Conclusion: Rocket Launch and the "Life Manual"

This transformation — from skill cultivation to real output — took over a year. But as the start date at the new company approaches, this rocket has completed its fuel loading. This launch is no longer blind propulsion, but a purposeful voyage.

The wasteland of ages 23 to 30 is far behind me. I no longer blame the environment, nor do I mock meaning. I've learned to translate "inner pursuits" into "real-world blueprints." Writing code is no longer just about honing technique — it's about hooking dreams to expected returns, using the "stability" of reality to earn the "wildness" of the soul.

The world is still absurd, and there are still many innate constraints. But in the inspiration of writing code and the aesthetics of designing interfaces, I've found my existence. I am no longer a wandering free spirit adrift in the wasteland — I am an **"Architect"** gradually building a tower.
