## I. Different Distances in a Single Burp

In the podcast "Summer Days Just Lying Home" EP52, a burp in the car sparked a debate on "natural" vs. "etiquette" in intimacy. One side argued the **"natural school"**—in front of the closest person, you should relax and be yourself; farting and burping are normal. The other side, the **"etiquette school"**, held that maintaining politeness is respect, and keeps romance alive.

I originally leaned natural. But with experience, I began to wonder: **Is trusting that your partner accepts your bodily functions actually self-indulgence and disrespect?** "Distance creates beauty"—perhaps with our closest person, keeping some distance is how we sustain the relationship.

## II. Kaeru-ka: The Fragility of Attraction

Attraction often rests on idealization. **When your partner shows raw bodily instincts without restraint, that moment can shatter the "refined individual" image. Suddenly you see "they're just another person who poops." It's not that they did wrong—it's the forced collapse of illusion. Like the prince turning back into a frog, the magic is gone.**

When one party carelessly shows "uncle/aunt-ification," it's not just about air quality and noise—it's about the filter in the other's mind. True maturity might mean considering more layers: **finding a balance between "being yourself" and "protecting your partner's idealization"** so that love doesn't cool.

## III. Is Performance Part of Love?

If a relationship requires running the tap to mask bathroom sounds, does love need packaging too? Or does love require some hypocrisy to exist?

Claude suggested that "focusing on etiquette" isn't hypocrisy—it's **"effort against instinct for the other's comfort."** In private, we could choose the easiest indulgence, but we choose restraint and moderation for our partner's experience. That "unnatural restraint" is the highest form of consideration.

Performance, by contrast, comes from different motivation: to create false promises, to pass off a facade as the real self. That can't last. **Mature consideration is real action for the other's sake—choosing the socialized "Superego" rather than the indulgent "Id."**

## IV. An "Essay Question," Not "True or False"

Claude reframed the "natural vs. etiquette" binary: it's not black and white. It depends on **context** and **mutual understanding**.

**The core isn't "can you fart?"—it's "are you consciously considering your partner's experience?"** Deliberately ignoring their comfort in the name of "being yourself" sends a dangerous signal: "I no longer care how you see me." In cultures that value boundaries (e.g., Japan, Germany), keeping distance in relationships isn't hypocrisy—it's respect for personal space.

## V. Conclusion: Distance Is Space, Longevity Is Time

Ultimately, this isn't a binary of "authentic" vs. "fake." **A mature relationship might not be two creatures letting it all hang out—it's two souls willing to temper their beastliness for each other.** If both understand, closeness or distance both work.

As one friend said, it's an "attitude" of consideration. I now lean toward "etiquette in private space"—not abandoning myself, but being aware of the other. **Setting boundaries isn't building walls; it's maintaining lasting space between us. Only with appropriate distance can beauty grow in the gaps.**
